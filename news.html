Hacktober Demo Day: a fenceline air watch for the well next door

October was HackFW's MADE Challenge, “Rebuilding System Density”: a month of virtual incubation from the October 1 kickoff to an in-person Enterprise Demo Day in Fort Worth on October 30.

Our build puts solar-powered ESP32 nodes around a well pad. Each one wakes, samples methane, H₂S and VOCs, and sends its readings over ESP-NOW to a Raspberry Pi gateway, which feeds a live site map. It's the cheap, always-on layer of our Texas well monitor: the part that watches the fence line when nobody is on site.

The well-pad example in our dashboard toolkit shows the site — pumpjack, tank battery, gate sensor and nodes — with a guided tour and scenario stories. Its data is simulated.

Oct 2026Field notes

Well Done Foundation's year of plugging orphan wells

The Well Done Foundation is a nonprofit that plugs orphaned and abandoned oil and gas wells — wells with no responsible operator left — and measures what each one emits before and after the plug goes in. It has plugged more than 117 wells in 15 states since its first, on Earth Day 2019, and it runs an orphaned wells program in Texas.

2026 has been its biggest year: 30 wells plugged in a single year at the Deep Fork National Wildlife Refuge in Oklahoma, a new field office in Okmulgee, a marine division for underwater and nearshore wells, field data in a peer-reviewed study of orphan-well methane, and new corporate backing.

Why we follow it: measurement decides which wells get plugged first and proves the plug worked. That is the job our well monitor is built to make cheap and continuous — commodity methane, H₂S and VOC sensors on a well before it's plugged, and on the same well afterwards.
