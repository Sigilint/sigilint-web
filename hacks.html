04 / Hacks — Proof, not slides

Three live hacks. Built by the team, on commodity parts.

Our POCs run in our homes, in a Texas field, and on a table at hackathons. They are deliberately low-fidelity — we optimize for end-to-end signal over demo polish. If it works on a carafe, it works on a pipeline.

POC-01

Factory Floor Simulation

● ACTIVE · TABLE-SCALE

A scale map of a factory floor — printed, laid on a folding table, populated with miniature machines. An RC car drives through, tripping badge (RFID) gates and motion sensors. A handheld Raspberry Pi, waved over the map, stands in for a patrol drone.

All signals publish to the Sigil Bus. Our laptop Sigil Watch shows the picture operators would see: badge events, motion detections, drone position fused into one timeline.

We run this at hackathons and at investor meetings. It fits in a backpack.

POC-02

Flow Carafe (“Glug-Glug”)

● ACTIVE · BENCHTOP

The cheapest convincing pipeline analog we could build: a commodity flow sensor on a coffee/water carafe, plus a microphone listening for the “glug-glug” of pouring. Both feed the Sigil Bus as if they were 40-inch pipeline segments.

It proves the whole stack — sensor → Pi node → DDS bus → fusion → Sigil Watch alert — on a single bench. We use it to stress-test QoS profiles and mesh recovery.

POC-03

Abandoned Well Monitor — Texas

● ACTIVE · FIELD-DEPLOYED

Tens of thousands of abandoned and orphan wells leak methane, H₂S, and worse across Texas — a hazard to livestock, a liability for the mineral lessor, and a regulatory exposure for the operator of record.

Our first outdoor deployment: solar-powered Sigil Nodes on a handful of abandoned wells on private land, publishing CH₄ / H₂S / VOC every few seconds. Data is available to the land-owner and their insurer. We treat it as a liability-reduction product first, environmental product second.

This is also our first paying pilot structure — fixed-fee, six-month engagement.
